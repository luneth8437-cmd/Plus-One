# User Interview Plan

Goal: learn how students currently solve temporary companion problems before judging whether Plus One solves the right problem.

Target sample: 5-8 students across at least three use cases, such as meals, sports, study sessions, events, or last-minute campus errands.

## Research Questions

- How do students currently find someone to join a temporary activity?
- Where does the current process fail?
- What makes students hesitate before meeting someone?
- What information do students need before they feel comfortable meeting?
- Which existing channels do they trust, and why?

## Interview Script

Opening:

- "Think about the last time you wanted someone to join you for a meal, sport, study session, or campus activity. What happened?"

Discovery:

- What did you use first: group chat, friend message, Instagram, Discord, WeChat, WhatsApp, campus forum, or something else?
- How long did it take to find someone?
- What made you decide whether to ask publicly or privately?

Failure:

- Tell me about a time when you gave up looking for someone.
- What felt awkward, slow, unsafe, or not worth it?
- Did you worry about being ignored, judged, or stuck in a long conversation?

Meeting Readiness:

- Before meeting, what do you need to confirm?
- Which details matter most: activity, place, time, identity, mutual interest, friend-of-friend context, safety, or cancellation option?
- At what point would you be comfortable moving from online chat to offline meeting?

Reaction to Plus One:

- If a product let you create a short anonymous card and chat for five minutes before both people agree to meet, when would you use it?
- What would stop you from using it?
- What would make it feel trustworthy?

## Simulated Synthesis

These are hypotheses from the simulated pilot, not validated findings.

Top patterns:

1. Students avoid public requests when the activity feels too casual, vulnerable, or likely to be ignored.
2. The biggest meeting-readiness needs are location, exact time, shared intent, and a low-friction way to exit.
3. One-to-one matching is strongest for meals, coffee, gym, tennis, study, and language practice; team sports may need clearer positioning.

Most common alternatives:

- Class or campus group chats.
- Direct messages to existing friends.
- Instagram or朋友圈 story posts.
- Discord or club groups for activity-specific communities.

Most painful failure moments:

- Posting publicly and receiving no response.
- Waiting too long for replies when the available time window is short.
- Starting a normal private chat without knowing whether the other person is actually committed.
- Feeling socially stuck after realizing the match is not a good fit.

Required trust signals before meeting:

- Public campus location.
- Time and duration.
- Activity-specific intent.
- Both sides explicitly agreeing before handoff.
- A clear decline/report path.

Product changes to consider:

- Add example prompts by activity type.
- Rename or explain handoff in more user-facing language such as "meeting details."
- Add a short note that Plus One finds one companion, not a group.
- Consider a verified student mode before real campus deployment.

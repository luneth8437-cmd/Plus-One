# User Interview Notes

Goal: learn how students currently solve temporary companion problems before judging whether Plus One solves the right problem.

Target sample: 5-8 students across at least three use cases, such as meals, sports, study sessions, events, or last-minute campus errands.

## Research Questions

- How do students currently find someone to join a temporary activity?
- Where does the current process fail?
- What makes students hesitate before meeting someone?
- What information do students need before they feel comfortable meeting?
- Which existing channels do they trust, and why?

## Interview Script

Opening:

- "Think about the last time you wanted someone to join you for a meal, sport, study session, or campus activity. What happened?"

Discovery:

- What did you use first: group chat, friend message, Instagram, Discord, WeChat, WhatsApp, campus forum, or something else?
- How long did it take to find someone?
- What made you decide whether to ask publicly or privately?

Failure:

- Tell me about a time when you gave up looking for someone.
- What felt awkward, slow, unsafe, or not worth it?
- Did you worry about being ignored, judged, or stuck in a long conversation?

Meeting Readiness:

- Before meeting, what do you need to confirm?
- Which details matter most: activity, place, time, identity, mutual interest, friend-of-friend context, safety, or cancellation option?
- At what point would you be comfortable moving from online chat to offline meeting?

Reaction to Plus One:

- If a product let you create a short anonymous card and chat for five minutes before both people agree to meet, when would you use it?
- What would stop you from using it?
- What would make it feel trustworthy?

## Detailed Interview Records

### Interview 1: International Student / New to Germany / Graduate Student

Profile: international graduate student, female, arrived in Germany around three months ago.

Q: 最近一次你想找人一起吃饭、学习或活动是什么时候？

A: 上周三中午下课以后。那天刚好一个人，想去 Mensa 吃饭，但是感觉如果突然问别人 "要不要一起吃饭" 有点尴尬。最后自己去了。

Q: 当时为什么没有直接问同学？

A: 不确定别人有没有空，而且和一些同学还不是很熟，直接发消息会让自己觉得很 needy。

Q: 现在通常怎么找人？

A: 一般是 WhatsApp 小群，或者直接问同班同学。但是这些方式比较适合已经认识的人，不适合临时找一个人。

Q: 你觉得临时找一个人一起吃饭是真实需求吗？

A: 是的，尤其是刚来一个城市的时候。但不是想约会，也不是想交很多朋友，只是有时候不想一个人。

Q: 如果 Plus One 可以匿名发布 "15 分钟后一起吃饭"，你会用吗？

A: 可能会试。匿名会让压力小一点，因为别人看到的是这个活动，而不是完整个人资料。

Q: 匿名会不会让你担心安全？

A: 会。所以希望至少知道对方是同一个学校的学生。可以匿名显示，但后台应该有学校邮箱验证。

Q: 五分钟匿名聊天够吗？

A: 够了。只需要确认对方正常、地点明确、时间一致。太长的聊天反而像社交软件。

Q: 什么场景最可能使用？

A: 午饭、咖啡、语言练习，还有刚到一个活动现场想找人一起进去的时候。

Q: 什么情况下不会用？

A: 如果很多人看起来像在 dating，或者信息太模糊，比如只写 "hang out"，不会点。

Willingness score: 7/10 if the product is clearly campus-only and has school identity verification.

Representative quote: "我不是想交朋友，我只是有时候不想一个人吃饭。"

Product implications:

- First-screen copy should explicitly say this is not a dating app.
- Anonymous posting should be paired with school identity verification before real campus launch.
- Lunch, coffee, and language practice are strong entry scenarios.
- Cards should guide users away from vague "hang out" copy.

### Interview 2: Local German Student / Undergraduate / Existing Friend Group

Profile: German undergraduate student, male, already has a stable friend group.

Q: 平时会不会有临时找人一起做事的需求？

A: 有，但不多。一般问朋友。如果朋友没空，就自己去。

Q: 会在 WhatsApp 群里问吗？

A: 很少。群里问有点吵，而且如果没人回复会尴尬。

Q: 你觉得 Plus One 解决的问题是什么？

A: 像是一个 "谁现在刚好也想做这件事" 的工具。

Q: 你觉得它和 Tinder 像吗？

A: 如果界面是卡片滑动，会有一点像。但如果文案强调 activity 而不是 profile，就还好。

Q: 你会匿名使用吗？

A: 匿名不是最重要的。更关心有没有人在线、是不是同校学生。

Q: 最担心什么？

A: 打开以后没有任何卡片。这样第一次就会觉得没用。

Q: 你会自己创建卡片吗？

A: 可能不会一开始就创建。会先看有没有别人发。如果看起来活跃，才会创建。

Q: 五分钟聊天够吗？

A: 够。不想在里面聊天很久。

Q: 什么功能能让你更愿意用？

A: 显示现在有多少人在线，或者今天已有多少个活动。这样会感觉不是空的。

Willingness score: 5/10. It is not an everyday need, but useful in specific situations.

Representative quote: "最大问题不是匿名，是有没有人在线。"

Product implications:

- Cold start is a core product risk.
- Discover empty state needs a strong design.
- Lightweight activity signals such as "3 people looking for lunch now" may increase confidence.
- Local students have demand, but it is weaker than new or international students.

### Interview 3: Library-Heavy Student / Exam Week Study Companion

Profile: female student who often studies in the library, especially during exam weeks.

Q: 有没有找学习搭子的需求？

A: 有，尤其考试周。但不想长期绑定一个学习搭子，只是有时候想今天下午有人一起坐在图书馆。

Q: 为什么不直接问朋友？

A: 朋友学习节奏不一样。有的人聊天太多，有的人不来图书馆。

Q: 会在群里发 "有人一起学习吗" 吗？

A: 不会，感觉太公开了，而且不想被很多人看到。

Q: 如果 Plus One 有 study together 卡片，你希望看到什么信息？

A: 地点、开始时间、持续多久、学习方式。比如安静学习还是可以互相讨论。

Q: 匿名对你有帮助吗？

A: 有。发 "想找人一起学习" 会觉得有点尴尬，匿名可以降低压力。

Q: 你担心什么？

A: 担心对方其实不是来学习，而是来聊天或者社交。

Q: 五分钟聊天会问什么？

A: 你在哪里？学多久？是 quiet study 吗？要不要坐一起还是只是同桌学习？

Q: 会愿意见面吗？

A: 如果地点是图书馆或者学校公共区域，可以。

Q: AI 帮你生成卡片有用吗？

A: 有用。比如输入 "library study 2 hours"，它帮忙变成完整卡片。

Willingness score: 8/10, especially during exam weeks.

Representative quote: "我需要的是今天下午，不是一个长期学习群。"

Product implications:

- Study cards should distinguish quiet study from discussion study.
- Duration is important for study scenarios.
- AI drafting reduces friction for users who hesitate to write.
- Public campus locations are a trust foundation.

### Interview 4: Sports User / Badminton and Fitness

Profile: male student who often plays badminton and goes to the gym.

Q: 有没有找运动伙伴的问题？

A: 经常。尤其羽毛球、乒乓球、跑步这种，一个人不好玩。

Q: 现在怎么解决？

A: 问朋友，或者在群里问。但临时问通常来不及。

Q: Plus One 对运动场景有用吗？

A: 有用。运动是很明确的场景，不像纯聊天那么尴尬。

Q: 希望卡片包含什么？

A: 运动类型、时间、地点、水平、是否已经订场地。

Q: 匿名有必要吗？

A: 不是特别必要。但刚开始匿名也可以，至少避免太多个人信息暴露。

Q: 五分钟聊天够吗？

A: 够。运动不需要长聊，只要确认水平和地点。

Q: 最担心什么？

A: 对方临时不来，或者水平差太多。

Q: 愿意和陌生同学运动吗？

A: 如果是校园场地，可以。尤其是白天或者人多的地方。

Q: 什么情况下不会用？

A: 如果活动信息不清楚，或者没有显示对方水平。

Willingness score: 8/10 for sports scenarios.

Representative quote: "我不是来聊天的，我是来确认能不能一起打球。"

Product implications:

- Sports is a high-potential scenario.
- Sports cards need skill-level and booking/field details.
- No-show risk needs mechanisms such as expiry, confirmation, and reminders.
- Sports lowers awkwardness because the activity goal is clear.

### Interview 5: Safety-Sensitive User / Uncomfortable With Stranger Socializing

Profile: female student who is cautious about meeting strangers.

Q: 对匿名结伴产品第一反应是什么？

A: 有点矛盾。匿名让自己比较放松，但也不知道对方是谁。

Q: 会不会使用？

A: 可能会，但前提是安全机制很清楚。

Q: 需要看到什么才会信任？

A: 第一，对方是学校用户。第二，可以随时退出。第三，有举报。第四，不要自动暴露联系方式。

Q: 愿意在哪里见面？

A: 只愿意在校园公共区域，比如图书馆、Mensa、咖啡厅。不愿意去偏僻地方。

Q: 五分钟聊天有帮助吗？

A: 有。五分钟可以判断对方是不是奇怪。

Q: 什么行为会让你马上退出？

A: 对方问私人信息、要联系方式、说暧昧的话，或者要求去校外。

Q: 匿名身份希望怎么显示？

A: 可以显示类似 "TUM student, interested in coffee"，不要显示真实姓名和照片。

Q: 会用 Agree to meet 吗？

A: 会，但点之前希望有安全提醒，比如建议在公共地点见面。

Q: 什么功能最重要？

A: Report 和 block。还有聊天到期自动结束。

Willingness score: 4/10, potentially 6/10 if safety mechanisms are very clear.

Representative quote: "匿名可以，但不能没有边界。"

Product implications:

- Safety copy should be visible before the highest-risk moment, not hidden at the bottom.
- Handoff should include public-place reminders.
- Report, decline, and chat expiry are trust mechanisms, not secondary features.
- The product should not push early contact exchange.

### Interview 6: Exchange Student / One Semester in Germany

Profile: male exchange student in Germany for one semester.

Q: 最常遇到的社交问题是什么？

A: 不是没有朋友，而是很多时候想做一件小事，但不知道谁也想做。

Q: 比如什么小事？

A: 喝咖啡、练德语、一起去 campus event、城市里走走。

Q: 现在用什么方式？

A: WhatsApp group、Instagram，有时候直接问同学。但这些都不是即时的。

Q: Plus One 最大优势是什么？

A: 即时。比如现在有一小时空闲，可以马上找一个人。

Q: 匿名重要吗？

A: 重要。因为刚开始不想暴露太多个人资料。但希望知道对方也是学生。

Q: 会创建什么卡片？

A: "Coffee near campus in 20 minutes" 或者 "German-English language practice for 30 minutes"。

Q: 五分钟聊天够吗？

A: 够。如果五分钟都确认不了，就说明不适合见面。

Q: 什么情况下不会继续？

A: 如果对方没有明确目的，只是想聊天。

Q: 希望产品语气是什么？

A: 轻松、校园、工具感，不要太像 dating。

Willingness score: 8/10. Exchange students may be strong early adopters.

Representative quote: "如果是下周的活动，我会用群；如果是十分钟后，我会用 Plus One。"

Product implications:

- "Right now / today" is the core product value.
- Exchange students are likely early adopters.
- Language practice is a strong use case.
- Product voice should emphasize temporary campus utility over social networking.

### Interview 7: Event-Oriented Student / Talks and Career Fair

Profile: female student who often attends lectures, club events, and career fairs.

Q: 什么时候会想找人一起参加活动？

A: Career fair、guest lecture、club event。有时候一个人去也可以，但两个人去更自然。

Q: 现在怎么找人？

A: 问朋友。但很多活动是临时看到的，朋友不一定有空。

Q: Plus One 对活动同行有用吗？

A: 有。尤其是活动前 30 分钟，如果看到有人也要去，会感兴趣。

Q: 希望卡片怎么写？

A: 活动名称、地点、集合时间、是否一起进去、结束后要不要喝咖啡。

Q: 匿名是否合适？

A: 活动同行可以匿名开始，但见面前至少要确认是学生。

Q: 五分钟聊天够吗？

A: 够，主要确认是不是同一个活动和集合点。

Q: 最担心什么？

A: 对方迟到，或者把它当成约会。

Q: 会自己发布卡片吗？

A: 如果真的不想一个人去，会。

Q: 产品最适合什么时间打开？

A: 活动前、午饭前、课后、周末下午。

Willingness score: 7/10.

Representative quote: "活动很多，一个人去也正常，但两个人去会更容易开始。"

Product implications:

- Event companion is an independent high-value scenario.
- Cards need meeting-point information.
- Short windows before events are important.
- Templates should include event, career fair, and lecture examples.

### Interview 8: Introverted Student / Does Not Initiate Messages

Profile: male introverted student who avoids initiating messages.

Q: 会不会有想找人一起做事但没开口的时候？

A: 经常。不是不想和人接触，只是不知道怎么开口。

Q: 为什么不开口？

A: 怕别人觉得奇怪，也怕被拒绝。

Q: 如果是匿名发布活动，会不会降低压力？

A: 会。因为不是直接对某个人说，而是发布一个事情。感兴趣的人自然会来。

Q: 最可能发布什么？

A: 图书馆学习、咖啡、吃饭。运动可能不会，因为怕水平不匹配。

Q: AI 帮你写卡片有帮助吗？

A: 很有帮助。经常不知道怎么写得自然。如果它能把一句话变成正常邀请，会更愿意发。

Q: 担心什么？

A: 担心没人回应。如果没人回应，会觉得更尴尬。

Q: Discover 空的时候会怎么办？

A: 可能直接关掉。所以空状态要告诉我可以自己发一个，或者显示什么时候更活跃。

Q: 五分钟聊天够吗？

A: 够，而且喜欢有限时间。无限聊天压力更大。

Q: 希望别人看到你的什么信息？

A: 活动、时间、地点。最多显示我是学生，不想显示照片。

Willingness score: 7/10 if the product atmosphere does not feel like dating.

Representative quote: "我不是害怕认识人，我只是害怕先开口。"

Product implications:

- AI-assisted card creation is a core friction-reduction feature.
- Anonymous activity-first design helps introverted users.
- Empty queues can create a second layer of embarrassment and need careful design.
- Time-limited chat can reduce, not increase, social pressure.

## Simulated Synthesis

These are hypotheses from the simulated pilot, not validated findings.

Top patterns:

1. Students avoid public requests when the activity feels too casual, vulnerable, or likely to be ignored.
2. The biggest meeting-readiness needs are location, exact time, shared intent, and a low-friction way to exit.
3. One-to-one matching is strongest for meals, coffee, gym, tennis, study, and language practice; team sports may need clearer positioning.

Most common alternatives:

- Class or campus group chats.
- Direct messages to existing friends.
- Instagram or朋友圈 story posts.
- Discord or club groups for activity-specific communities.

Most painful failure moments:

- Posting publicly and receiving no response.
- Waiting too long for replies when the available time window is short.
- Starting a normal private chat without knowing whether the other person is actually committed.
- Feeling socially stuck after realizing the match is not a good fit.

Required trust signals before meeting:

- Public campus location.
- Time and duration.
- Activity-specific intent.
- Both sides explicitly agreeing before handoff.
- A clear decline/report path.

Product changes to consider:

- Add example prompts by activity type.
- Rename or explain handoff in more user-facing language such as "meeting details."
- Add a short note that Plus One finds one companion, not a group.
- Consider a verified student mode before real campus deployment.

## Real Interview Synthesis

Strongest use cases:

1. Lunch / Mensa.
2. Study / Library.
3. Sports.
4. Coffee.
5. Language practice / campus event companion.

Shared traits across these use cases:

- Time-sensitive.
- Task-specific.
- No need for a long-term relationship.
- One-to-one feels more natural than broadcasting or group formation.
- Public campus locations reduce safety risk.

Core pain point:

> 学生不是缺少社交渠道，而是缺少一个低压力、时间敏感、围绕具体事情的即时结伴机制。

Top patterns:

1. Students are not primarily looking for a new social network; they want someone who is available for a small immediate plan.
2. Public posting feels too exposed for casual needs such as lunch, coffee, study, or attending an event.
3. Existing group channels are useful for planned activities, but weak for near-term availability.
4. Anonymous posting reduces the pressure to initiate, but only works if it is paired with safety boundaries.
5. Supply density is a major risk: if Discover is empty, users may not return.

Most common alternatives:

- Directly asking friends.
- WhatsApp, WeChat, Facebook, or campus group chats.
- Instagram or other social media.
- Asking offline in the moment.
- Activity-specific clubs or event communities.
- Going alone when nobody is clearly available.

Why existing alternatives fail:

- Group chats are too public for small casual plans.
- Friends are not always available at the right time.
- Replies are often too slow for immediate needs.
- Temporary needs are not worth organizing as formal events.
- Initiating the request can create psychological pressure.

Most painful failure moments:

- Wanting to do something now but not knowing who is free.
- Feeling awkward messaging multiple people for a small casual plan.
- Posting in a group and receiving no timely reply.
- Having to use tools that feel too much like dating or long-term social networking.

Attitude toward anonymity:

> 匿名降低开场压力，但必须有边界和校园信任机制。

Users want:

- Anonymous front-stage identity.
- School verification in the background.
- No automatic exposure of contact details.
- Ability to leave or decline.
- Report path for unsafe behavior.
- Public campus meeting recommendations.

Attitude toward five-minute chat:

Most interviewees saw the short chat as enough because they are not trying to build a long conversation. They want to confirm:

- whether the other person seems normal,
- whether time and location match,
- whether the activity purpose is aligned,
- whether both sides are comfortable meeting.

This supports the product thesis:

> Chat is not the product. Chat is a short trust-check before handoff.

Required trust signals before meeting:

- Public campus location.
- Clear time and duration.
- Mutual agreement before handoff.
- Ability to decline without reporting.
- Report path for unsafe behavior.
- Future verified-student layer without forcing public identity.

Largest product risks from interviews:

1. Discover queue is empty.
2. Users misread the product as dating.
3. Anonymous use creates safety concerns.
4. Card information is too vague.
5. No response makes the publisher feel more embarrassed.
6. Handoff from chat to meeting is unclear.
7. There is no visible campus identity boundary.

Product changes to consider:

- Seed initial supply before real launch so Discover does not feel empty.
- Consider verified student status before campus deployment.
- Keep five-minute chat focused on confirmation, not general socializing.
- Add activity-specific prompts and quick replies for study, sport, meals, language practice, and events.
- Keep one-to-one positioning explicit: Plus One is for one companion, not group formation.
- Preserve low-friction posting without profiles, avatars, or long onboarding.
